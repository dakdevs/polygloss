## The viewport's own row motion

- The model change is already cheap: `Document::set_collapsed` changes one file's height in the Fenwick `HeightIndex` (O(log n), `document/mod.rs:614-626`); `set_section_open` relayouts the section's slots (`document/sections.rs:87-102`, budgeted by `section_toggle_ms` < 50).
- Each frame's display list is built in prepaint from the visible slots (`paint_rows.rs:1-18`, `view.rs:802-913`), and hitboxes and controls come from it (`element.rs:87-120`).
- Technique (ADR-0030 M3–M8): commit the final model at once; keep a transient reveal `{slot, h0, h1, start}`; each frame paint the changing body clipped by a curtain at its top + h(t), with D = min(abs(h1 − h0), viewport height) and h(t) = h0 + D · s (expanding) or h1 + D · (1 − s) (collapsing); the card's frame (bottom border, corners, shadow) closes at the curtain and every later slot is painted at its committed y + h(t) − h1, so the curtain and the cards below always agree and whatever the clamp skips lies below the viewport. The body's rows keep their pre-commit screen y. Hit-testing, the cursor and the scroll anchor stay exact because they come from the committed model and the shifted frame. A tick costs one ordinary viewport frame plus the chrome.
- Rejected: writing the animated height into the `HeightIndex` each frame (fights the anchor and wrap corrections). `prepare_window` skips collapsed files (`view.rs` ≈ L960), so a file collapsed before it was laid out (auto-collapsed, Viewed, restored) has an estimated height: it is laid out and shaped on the commit frame of its expand, which `collapse_commit_ms` bounds. Only a body whose data is not loaded snaps.
- A pinned header's collapse moves the anchor to the header; keeping the rows that were on screen at their screen y under the curtain while the next card rises from the viewport's bottom animates it without a jump (ADR-0030 M3, OQ-63; it snaps if its filmstrip shows one).

## Testing and recording

- Executor-clock motion is sampled deterministically: `advance_clock`, refresh, park, read bounds (`debug_bounds`) or a recording seam (`motion::record_entrances` today).
- Filmstrips: `HeadlessAppContext` `advance_clock` + `capture_screenshot` at the commit frame, the next three 60 Hz frames, ½ and 1 of the duration, one contact sheet per motion and policy. Sampling at quarters of the duration hides an ease-out's first frames: with `(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` the ¼, ½ and ¾ frames are 83%, 97% and 99.8% of the way.
- Review: frames every 16.67 ms of executor time, encoded with ffmpeg, then ui-recording-timeline (`extract`, `candidates`, `sheet`; one animation counts as one paint whose base is the frame before it).
- Wall-clock kit motion (dialogs, toasts, tooltips) can only be recorded from the real app (`screencapture -v`, then the timeline tool).

## Performance notes

- The latest results (`benches/results/2026-10-05-7c9475e3ac0c.json`, M3 Max): bare-viewport scroll CPU p95 0.32–1.09 ms, scroll max up to 8.85 ms (Linux), presented-frame interval p95 10.2–14.3 ms.
- No metric covers the full app shell: the perf harness opens a bare viewport window (`polygloss-perf src/harness.rs:1-13`). gpui-kit's `profiler` feature exposes `FrameTimingCollector` (`gpui-pre profiler.rs:788-848,1225-1262`); the app already runs `--perf-scenario` (`polygloss-app/src/perf/mod.rs`). T7.3 adds a zero-dependency render-to-paint sentinel instead.
- Budgets and targets ADR-0030 adopts: full-app animation frame p95 < 8.3 ms and max < 16.7 ms; viewport-only p95 < 8.3 ms; a panel's commit frame < 16.7 ms typical and < 50 ms Linux; a card collapse's < 16.7 ms. Reported, not gated: the chrome tax (`shell_idle_draw_p95_ms`, target ≤ 2 ms; above it, cache the main column under modal overlays) and animation frames ≤ 4 ms on the M3 Max.
- Hazards: a clock started at input time loses the first 20–40% of a short motion to a heavy commit (a layout switch's `set_metrics`); per-frame shaping of revealed rows (shape them on the commit frame; later frames must shape nothing); a cached view notified every frame; two copies painted for a crossfade.

## Reduce Motion notes

- Reduced variants are opacity only, ≤ 150 ms in and ≤ 100 ms out; transforms jump to their end; disclosures snap the layout and fade the revealed content. A panel or disclosure close fades its content out in the frame or slot it still holds, then snaps: fading after the snap would overlap what took its place, and GPUI has no group opacity.
- They need an app-owned sampler: gpui-base settles on frame 1 under `App::reduce_motion()`.
- The Off policy for harnesses is separate: writing `cx.set_reduce_motion` from production would stop gpui-base following the system.
- Live switching: unverified whether the Accessibility Shortcuts panel (⌥⌘F5) deactivates the app; the notification observer covers it either way. T7.15 checks it by hand. The app's `ReduceMotionSource` already routes reads through gpui-base's `apply_system_reduce_motion` (`motion.rs:108-136`), which writes `App::reduce_motion` with its own bookkeeping, so one live read serves app and kit motion.
- Harnesses: any policy override also sets `App::reduce_motion`, so the kit's wall-clock dialog entrance (`dialog.rs:607,752-790`) settles in every filmstrip, whatever app policy it samples.
- Kit motion under Reduce Motion snaps (no fade-only path); the kit Spinner goes static and keeps its text label.
